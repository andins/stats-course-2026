# Core references & citation discipline

## Citation discipline (read before citing anything)

1. **Never invent a page number.** If you don't know the exact page, cite by chapter/section title, or author+year+title only. Only give a page number if you've verified it in this conversation (e.g. via `fetch_webpage` on a publisher/preview page) or are highly confident from well-known structure.
2. **Prefer primary sources for strong claims** (a specific paper) over "the blog" in general. Andrew Gelman's blog (statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu) is a great pointer for *framing and critique*, but the load-bearing citation for a specific statistical claim should usually be the underlying paper or textbook.
3. **If asked for a specific blog post** you don't already know the URL/date of, use `fetch_webpage` to search statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu rather than guessing a URL — don't fabricate a link.
4. **Distinguish "well-established textbook fact" from "one researcher's recommendation."** E.g. "Welch's t-test is generally preferred over Student's" is a widely replicated recommendation (cite Delacre et al.) — present it as a recommendation with its source, not as uncontested law.

## How to use this table in an answer

Reference by key inline, e.g. "Welch's test is the recommended default (DELACRE_LAKENS_LEYS, 2017)", then expand to the full citation in the References section of the answer using the table above. Only add a chapter/section/page locator when you're confident it's correct.

## Locators used in the companion course's "Learn more" boxes

Each page of the companion course ends with a "Learn more" box. Page numbers there were checked against the free PDFs: *Regression and Other Stories* (avehtari.github.io/ROS-Examples, 2020-2024 PDF), *Causal Inference: What If* (Aug 2026 PDF, miguelhernan.org/whatifbook) and *An Introduction to Statistical Learning* 2nd ed. (June 2023 corrected PDF). Pagination of other printings may differ by a few pages. Other books are cited by chapter or section only, because their page numbers could not be verified; journal articles use the page ranges from the table above or the published record. The Cochrane Handbook chapter numbers (10 and 13) were checked against the table of contents of version 6.5 online.
