Rules of thumb that apply at every node

  • Lead with the estimate and its confidence interval, not a bare significant/non-significant verdict — true even in the best-case confirmatory/experimental branch. See P-values and significance.
  • Design-phase mistakes (noise, missing variables) can't be fixed by a better model later — get instrument choice and variable coverage right before collecting data. See Design considerations.
  • A well-fitting model is not automatically a causal one — causal claims need an identification strategy (experiment or DAG-based adjustment), not just good fit. See Prediction vs. explanation.
  • A predictive model isn't done once it validates well — the final step is serving its predictions on genuinely new data, and that deployment setting should keep being checked against the out-of-sample performance it was validated on. See Cross-validation and model checking.
  • One study is rarely the last word — when several studies answer the same question, combine their estimates (not their significant/non-significant verdicts), report how much they disagree, and say how they were selected and what may be missing. See Meta-analysis and Meta-analysis: bias and pitfalls.
