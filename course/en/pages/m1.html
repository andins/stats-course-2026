Module 1: Planning Your Study

Before you collect data, there is one question to answer: how many samples do you need?

This module covers two versions of that question and how to answer them.

Pages

  1. Sample size and power — the standard "how many mice/cells/patients do I need" question.
  2. Effect size — the number every sample-size calculation depends on, and why it is difficult to estimate.
  3. Other design-phase considerations — reducing measurement noise upfront, and making sure the design captures what a causal question will need.

Why this comes first

It's tempting to collect "as much data as is feasible" and figure out the analysis later. The problem: a study that's too small can't reliably detect a real effect even if one exists (it's underpowered), while a study that's much larger than needed wastes time, money, and — in animal or human research — subjects. Planning sample size is also an ethical question, not just a statistical one. In addition, a big sample size can have enough power to detect even trivially small effects, which may not be scientifically meaningful. So, in essence, plan ahead and choose a sample size that balances feasibility, ethics, and the ability to detect meaningful effects.
