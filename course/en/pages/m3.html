Module 3: Hypothesis Testing

The standard toolkit: t-tests, ANOVA, chi-squared tests, and related methods. This module explains what they claim, what they assume, and where they are commonly misused.

Pages

  1. P-values and significance — what a p-value means (and the two things it is commonly confused with).
  2. Common tests — a quick-reference tour of t-tests, ANOVA, chi-squared, correlation, and nonparametric alternatives.
  3. Checking assumptions — every test has fine-print conditions; here's how to check them without overthinking it.
  4. Multiple comparisons — what happens when you run more than one test at once.

A reminder

Everything in this module assumes you're in confirmatory territory, ideally a well-designed experiment — see Exploratory vs. confirmatory analysis if you haven't settled that question yet. Even then, treat the significant/non-significant verdict as the least useful part of the output — the effect-size estimate and its confidence interval (see P-values and significance) do the real work.
