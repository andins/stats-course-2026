# Module 3: Hypothesis Testing

The standard toolkit: t-tests, ANOVA, chi-squared tests, and related methods. This module explains what they claim, what they assume, and where they are commonly misused.

## Pages

1. [P-values and significance](./p-values-and-significance.md) — what a p-value means (and the two things it is commonly confused with).
2. [Common tests](./common-tests.md) — a quick-reference tour of t-tests, ANOVA, chi-squared, correlation, and nonparametric alternatives.
3. [Checking assumptions](./checking-assumptions.md) — every test has fine-print conditions; here's how to check them without overthinking it.
4. [Multiple comparisons](./multiple-comparisons.md) — what happens when you run more than one test at once.

## A reminder

Everything in this module assumes you're in **confirmatory** territory, ideally a well-designed experiment — see [Exploratory vs. confirmatory analysis](../02-exploring-data/exploratory-vs-confirmatory.md) if you haven't settled that question yet. Even then, treat the significant/non-significant verdict as the least useful part of the output — the effect-size estimate and its confidence interval (see [P-values and significance](./p-values-and-significance.md)) do the real work.
